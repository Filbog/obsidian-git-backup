Electronic 1st curtain is like a hybrid between mechanical and electronic shutter. In most of the scenarios, best to use this one. 
We want to use **electronic** if:
 - we need super fast shutter speeds
 - we need maximum FPS burst on our cameras
 - we want a completely silent camera
However, using electronic has downsides:
 - potentially reducing the dynamic range
 - you can't shoot at very slow shutter speeds like 1s 

### Reddit post
The two types of shutters available on the R8 are Electronic Shutter (ES) and Electronic First Curtain (EFCS), where the second curtain is mechanical. You have to move up to the R6/R5 to also get a Full Mechanical shutter mode (i.e. first and second curtains being mechanical).

In ES, you have an option of a silent mode or a fake shutter sound. In EFCS, you get the mechanical sound.

There are differences between ES and EFCS. Trying not to forget anything:

- ES can shoot at 40fps vs. 6fps in EFCS.
    
- ES can be fully silent.
    
- ES max shutter speed is 1/16,000s in some modes (and 1/8,000s in the others) vs. 1/4,000s in EFCS.
    
- ES does not use the mechanical shutter, so there is no long-term reliability issue with the mechanical shutter.
    
- Out of focus bright lights (bokeh balls) can have truncated shapes in EFCS at high shutter speeds and large apertures lenses. ES is also not perfect (vs. full mechanical shutter, which is not an option on the R8), but not as visible. Not an issue in most cases (source: Canon R8 manual below and [this DPReview thread on EFCS bokeh balls on Sony cameras](https://www.dpreview.com/forums/thread/4280705)).
    

BUT

- ES won't work with flash, Dual-pixel RAW, Anti-flicker, Shooting Creative Filters, High-Speed Display (original source with multiple errors: [Ken Rockwell](https://www.kenrockwell.com/canon/eos-r/r8.htm#spex) - thanks [u/Specific-Fuel-4366](https://www.reddit.com/user/Specific-Fuel-4366/) for the corrections - I rechecked all those on my R8).
    
- Rolling shutter is 14.5ms in ES vs. 3.4ms in EFCS. This can be important to avoid deformation of fast-moving subjects (source: [The Digital Picture](https://www.the-digital-picture.com/Reviews/Canon-EOS-R8.aspx#FrameRate,BufferDepth,Shutter)).
    
- RAW files are 12b in ES and 14b in EFCS. That should be minor for most people unless the picture is very under-exposed. This is the case on many other cameras, including the R5 and R6.
    
- ES may show some banding with flickering lights.
    

The Canon R8 manual has a (very partial) list of limitations of ES and EFCS. See the "Caution" box on this page: [https://cam.start.canon/en/C013/manual/html/UG-04_Shooting-1_0370.html](https://cam.start.canon/en/C013/manual/html/UG-04_Shooting-1_0370.html)
